\begin{abstract}
For a nontrivial root of unity $\zeta$, put $K_\zeta=\Q(\zeta)$ and
\[
 F_\zeta(T)=T^6+2T^5+4T^4+6T^3+(\zeta-5)T^2-8T-16.
\]
We strengthen the period-$4$ Morton--Vivaldi irreducibility theorem by
proving that $F_\zeta$ remains irreducible over the maximal abelian
extension $K_\zeta^{\mathrm{ab}}$.  Equivalently, if $\alpha$ is a
root, then
\[
 K_\zeta(\alpha)\cap K_\zeta^{\mathrm{ab}}=K_\zeta.
\]
It follows that, over every finite abelian extension of $\Q$, the
factorization of the period-$4$ pullback is caused entirely by the
cyclotomic polynomial: there is no additional splitting inside a
degree-six fiber.  We also determine the maximal abelian subfield of
each exact-period-$4$ parabolic parameter field.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The companion paper \cite{WeiPeriod4} proves the irreducibility of the
period-$4$ cyclotomic pullbacks over $\Q$ and, through the multiplier-curve
normalization, settles the complete period-$4$ case of the Morton--Vivaldi
irreducibility conjecture.  The present paper asks a stronger field-theoretic
question about each degree-six fiber: can it split after an arbitrary abelian
extension of its cyclotomic coefficient field?  Theorem
\ref{thm:abelian-stability} answers this question negatively.

The proof has two local ingredients at primes above $2$.  First, the fiber
$F_\zeta$ has an irreducible quartic factor over a completion of
$\Q(\zeta)$.  Second, an explicit $3+3$ resolvent has no root in
$\Q(\zeta)$.  An abstract criterion for irreducible sextics then excludes
every possible nontrivial abelian subfield of a root field.  The displayed
resolvent and the three congruences used in its local analysis are certified
by deterministic exact-integer computation.

The next sections develop the Newton-polygon input, the local quartic factor,
the abstract sextic criterion, and the explicit resolvent calculation.  The
abelian-stability theorem then follows by combining these ingredients.  The
remaining sections describe factorization over abelian base fields, transfer
the result to the period-$4$ delta factors, determine maximal abelian
subfields of the corresponding parameter fields, and place the result in the
context of torsion Hilbert irreducibility.

\section{Set-up and established inputs}

Define
\[
 g(T)=-T^4-2T^3-4T^2-6T+5+\frac8T+\frac{16}{T^2}.
\]
For every root of unity $\zeta$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:draft-g-F}
 g(T)-\zeta=-\frac{F_\zeta(T)}{T^2}.
\end{equation}
The companion period-$4$ paper proves that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:established-base-irreducibility}
 F_\zeta\text{ is irreducible over }\Q(\zeta)
 \qquad(\zeta\ne1).
\end{equation}
We shall also use the local facts proved there at primes above
$2$: odd-order roots of unity reduce injectively, $2$-power roots of
unity reduce to $1$, and the ramification index in the $2$-power
cyclotomic part has the values recalled below.  In the case of a
nontrivial odd part, the exact reduction and special-value valuation
used in \eqref{eq:quartic-established-odd-data} were proved in the base
irreducibility argument.  No local factor of degree four was asserted
there; that new conclusion is proved here.  These established inputs are
recorded in \cite{WeiPeriod4}.

\section{Abelian stability}

\begin{theorem}[Abelian-stable irreducibility]
\label{thm:abelian-stability}
Let $\zeta\ne1$ be any root of unity and put $K=\Q(\zeta)$.  Then
\[
 F_\zeta(T)\text{ is irreducible over }K^{\mathrm{ab}}.
\]
Equivalently, if $\alpha$ is a root of $F_\zeta$, then
\[
 [K(\alpha):K]=6,
 \qquad
 K(\alpha)\cap K^{\mathrm{ab}}=K.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The base irreducibility statement
\eqref{eq:established-base-irreducibility} gives
$[K(\alpha):K]=6$.  Since $K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K$ is Galois, the standard
degree formula gives
\[
 [K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\alpha):K^{\mathrm{ab}}]
 = [K(\alpha):K(\alpha)\cap K^{\mathrm{ab}}].
\]
Thus the stated intersection equality is equivalent to irreducibility
over $K^{\mathrm{ab}}$.  Proposition
\ref{prop:local-quartic-factor} supplies an irreducible quartic local
factor of $F_\zeta$.  Equation \eqref{eq:res-no-K-root} proves that the
$3+3$ resolvent has no root in $K$.  Theorem
\ref{thm:sextic-abelian-disjointness} now gives both assertions.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact symbolic certificates}
\label{sec:exact-certificates}

The verification directory contains an exact certificate using integer
polynomial arithmetic and no third-party dependencies.  It constructs the
companion matrix of $P_X$, constructs the matrix of its third exterior power
from all $3\times3$ minors, and verifies every coefficient of the
exterior-power resolvent identity and the displayed resolvent formula.  It
also verifies the three polynomial congruences coefficient by coefficient.
The program exits with a nonzero status on any failed equality or nonexact
integer division; no floating-point computation or numerical specialization
occurs.

From the directory containing this draft, the certificate is run with the
command
\begin{quote}
\noindent python3 verification/verify\_resolvent.py
\end{quote}
